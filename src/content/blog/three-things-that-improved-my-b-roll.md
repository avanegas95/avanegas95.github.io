I'm not a full-time filmmaker. I shoot B-roll for personal projects and to learn editing. These three habits helped more than buying another lens.

## 1. Shoot for the edit, not the moment

Before pressing record, I ask: *What cut does this support?* One wide establishing shot beats five similar pans. Variety (wide, medium, detail) saves hours in post.

## 2. Hold every shot five seconds longer

My instinct is to stop as soon as the action ends. Holding an extra five seconds gives handles for transitions and music beats. Most clips I used to trash became usable.

## 3. Stabilize in-camera first

Software stabilization helps, but cropping hurts resolution. Slower movement, two hands, or a simple rig beats fixing shake in DaVinci Resolve.

## Takeaway

Better B-roll is mostly **intention and patience**, not gear. I still break these rules — but when I follow them, editing feels fun instead of like salvage work.

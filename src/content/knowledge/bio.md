### Who he is

Anderson Vanegas is a Staff SQA Engineer at Boston Dynamics, based in the Boston area, MA. He has worked in software quality assurance since 2019.

His day job is making sure robot software behaves before it meets a real robot. He also builds web projects with AI-assisted tools.

### What he does

- Plans and organizes test suites so teams know what to run, and when.
- Writes Python and Appium automation for UI and hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) testing.
- Triages nightly robot test runs and finds out why things failed.
- Mentors and onboards teammates on QA practices.
- Builds web projects with AI-assisted tools, using React, Vite, FastAPI and Supabase.

### Where he has worked

- Boston Dynamics: Staff SQA Engineer since March 2026, after serving as Senior SQA Engineer from September 2023.
- SharkNinja: SQA Engineer, then SQA II, from November 2019 to September 2023.

### How did you get into QA and testing?

I started off in SharkNinja as a Product Development Intern and worked very closely with the SQA team there. After continuously getting my contract extended a few times, I asked to join the SQA team as they had an open role. Since they knew my work ethic, I had a quick informal interview and joined shortly after!

### What do you love most about QA? What's the most underrated part of it?

What I love most is improving the end product. You know that you helped build and have input that helped push the product in a way that helps the customer and is easier and more intuitive to use. You help make sure it isn't annoying to use, it doesn't cause friction. It all just works and makes lives easier. You're their voice and you advocate for them.

### A bug you're proud of finding?

There's two. One was a behavior on a Shark robot vacuum. It had a sensor that when it reached an 80% threshold of particulates picked up, would initiate a spot clean and go over that area, in a corn-row pattern a few times. However it only did it once per room, but the code and devs kept saying it should do it multiple times per room. It was tough to test as it took a lot of setup, time, and hope. Through a lot of persistence, videos, and having a dev run through it with me, they caught the issue and were able to fix the logic.

Another one would be an issue people were seeing internally which was a connection issue, but no one reported it. So I went ahead and made a high priority ticket, with details, videos, logs and sparked discussions investigations. As a tester I felt frustrated that it took so much effort to connect. That is not the feeling I want a consumer to feel so I will gladly bang my head against a wall figuring it out so they don't have to.

### A testing hot take or opinion you'll defend?

Take the time upfront to be as explicit as possible to remove all ambiguity. This avoids confusion and variables from your testers or testers who execute your work. It helps reproducibility which helps save time in the long run.
